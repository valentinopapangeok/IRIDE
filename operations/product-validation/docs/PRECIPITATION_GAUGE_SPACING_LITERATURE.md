# Gauge spacing in point-to-area precipitation validation studies

Reviewed 28 October 2026 for S5-02-04. This is a versioned reading note with links to the source publications, not a copy of their full texts. It distinguishes **station separation**, **gauges per study pixel**, **grid size**, and **network density**; those are different quantities. Where a paper did not give an accessible numeric station-separation range, no range is inferred from its grid size or density.

## Consequence for our baseline

The strongest like-for-like hourly **4 km** reference design found here is Villarini et al.'s two 4 × 4 km Brue cells with **12–13 gauges each and at least 10 simultaneous valid readings**. By contrast, our initial cached S5-02-04 audit found mostly one gauge per delivered pixel and only isolated pixels with **three to five distinct station sites**; those counts are not equivalent to the Brue reference design. The Peleg study's three-site criterion applies to its **much smaller** studied footprint. No reviewed paper supplies a universal maximum gauge separation that, by itself, certifies an hourly 4 km areal mean or licenses a fixed percentage deduction from rRMSE.

The local audit below measures **actual pairwise separations** for repeated S5-02-04 clusters, treating near-coincident gauges as one possible site. The original operational KPI and any proposed representativeness-adjusted score must remain separately labelled until an error model is independently calibrated and validated.

## Local gauge-geometry audit, 28 October 2026

**Later 28 October cache update:** MeteoHub request 5624189 added the 20 October 23:00 UTC comparison. The expanded audit now has 43 hourly comparisons on 14 dates and 32 rainy pixel-hours with at least three sites, but still only **12 distinct station configurations**. Four dense observations revisit the Lombardia, Veneto, Campania and Sicilia pockets; they add temporal evidence, not a new geographic calibration site. The local `pixel_hour_clusters.csv` and `TRANSFER_TEST.md` were refreshed. The table below records the original 42-hour baseline for provenance.

The follow-up audit used the 42 distinct cached hourly `matched_all_conditions.json` comparisons in `validation_app/rain_rainy_only_campaign_20261022/results/S5-02-04`, spanning 13 dates. It grouped gauge observations by actual product raster row/column and hour, then treated sites closer than 100 m as one possible location. Great-circle distances were calculated between the remaining coordinates. The detailed, non-repository cache artifact is `validation_app/rain_gauge_geometry_20261028/pixel_hour_clusters.csv`; each row retains the input artifact path and station IDs. Counts describe **matched gauges in cached comparisons**, not every gauge known to the provider, and repeated pixel-hours are not independent spatial network layouts.

| Same-product-cell support | All valid gauge conditions | Positive-rain gauges only |
|---|---:|---:|
| Matched pixel-hours | 43,566 | 6,636 |
| One distinct site | 42,302 (97.1%) | 6,402 (96.5%) |
| Two distinct sites | 1,061 (2.4%) | 206 (3.1%) |
| At least three distinct sites | 203 (0.47%) | 28 (0.42%) |
| Maximum distinct sites in one cell-hour | 5 | 4 |
| Unique station configurations with at least three sites | 19 | 12 |

The **28 rainy cell-hours** occur on 7, 8, 10, 19 and 21 October: **6 North, 8 Center and 14 South**. They comprise 25 three-site and three four-site observations. Among their **12 unique station configurations**, the farthest pair within a cell ranges from **1.47 to 3.83 km** (median **2.34 km**); the closest pair ranges from **0.14 to 2.04 km** (median **1.40 km**). Thus some sites are adequately separated to be distinct, but the repeated dense cells cover a very small fraction of rainy matched pixel-hours. The station convex hull is also sometimes narrow or nearly collinear, so three sites do not necessarily sample a cell broadly.

These observed separations overlap those in Peleg and Zhang, but **spacing alone is insufficient**: their network layouts and studied footprints differ. Our cells never reach the 10 simultaneously valid gauges used for Villarini et al.'s hourly 4 × 4 km areal reference. The local audit establishes a sparse baseline for testing a representativeness model; it does not by itself quantify uncertainty or justify deducting a fixed amount from the operational rRMSE.
